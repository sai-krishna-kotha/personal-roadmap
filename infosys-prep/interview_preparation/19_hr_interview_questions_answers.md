## How to Use These Answers

[Back to Table of Contents](#table-of-contents)

These answers are written in simple, natural English.

Do not memorize every sentence word for word.

The goal is to remember the main idea and say it naturally.

Good HR answers should sound like:
- calm
- honest
- simple
- positive
- direct

Do not try to use big words just to sound professional.

## TCS Offer Questions

[Back to Table of Contents](#table-of-contents)

This is the most important special section for your situation.

You already have a TCS Digital offer. The interviewer may ask this directly.

### You already have a TCS Digital offer. Why are you attending this interview?

"I wanted to keep my options open and attend the interview because the role here is also very relevant to the kind of work I want to do. I don't want to choose a company only because I already have an offer. I want to understand the role, the kind of work I can get and where I can learn more. So I decided to attend the process seriously."

This is better than saying:
- "I don't like TCS."
- "Infosys pays more."
- "I am only here for a better package."
- "I will join whichever company gives more money."

### Are you going to leave TCS if Infosys selects you?

"I already have a TCS offer, so I have thought about it seriously. At the same time, I am attending this interview because I want to understand this opportunity properly. I would make the final decision based on the role, learning opportunities, work and long-term fit."

### Why should we select you when you already have an offer?

"I think the offer does not reduce my interest in this role. I am here because I am genuinely interested in the opportunity. I have prepared seriously for the technical side, and I believe I can learn quickly and contribute once I understand the team's work."

### Why not just join TCS?

"TCS is a good opportunity for me and I respect that offer. But having one offer does not mean I should stop exploring opportunities that are also relevant to my career. I wanted to understand this role properly before making my final decision."

### Are you using Infosys only as a backup?

"No. I am treating this as a genuine opportunity. I would like to understand the role, team, technology and growth path before deciding."

### What if Infosys gives you a lower role than the one you expected?

"I would first understand the actual work, responsibilities and growth path. I would not decide only from the title. The kind of work and learning opportunity are important to me."

### What if TCS gives you a joining date before Infosys completes the process?

"I would handle the timelines carefully and be transparent. My goal is to make a proper decision after I understand the opportunities available to me."

### If selected, will you definitely join Infosys?

"I am genuinely interested in the opportunity, and I would like to discuss the role and understand the final offer. I don't want to make a false promise before seeing the complete details, but I am taking this process seriously."

### Why did you not reject the TCS offer before attending?

"Because I don't see a reason to close one genuine opportunity before I understand another one completely. I wanted to keep my options open and make a thoughtful decision."

### Are you only interested because Infosys has a higher package for some roles?

"Compensation is one part of a job, but it is not the only thing I look at. Role, work, learning and long-term growth matter to me too."

### What if TCS offers you a better package later?

"I would compare the complete opportunities at that time, not just the package. I would look at the role, work, growth and learning as well."

### What do you expect from Infosys?

"I am mainly looking for a role where I can work on real software problems, improve my technical skills and take more responsibility over time. I would also like to work with technologies that match my interests in backend development, AI and cloud."

### Why should we believe you will stay?

"I am looking for a place where I can grow for the long term. My focus is not on changing companies quickly. I want to become strong in my work first and take bigger responsibilities over time."

## Behavioral Questions

[Back to Table of Contents](#table-of-contents)

### Tell me about a difficult situation you faced.

"One difficult situation for me was dealing with a project where different parts of the system had to work together, including APIs, databases, background jobs and AI services. There were issues that did not come from one place. I had to break the problem into smaller parts, test each part and find where the actual issue was. That taught me not to guess and instead debug step by step."

### Tell me about a failure.

"I have had situations where I did not get the result I expected, including in coding and placement-related situations. Initially it was disappointing, but I tried to understand what I did wrong. The main lesson for me was that preparation is not only about knowing concepts; I also need to perform correctly under the actual test conditions."

### Tell me about a time you disagreed with someone.

"I try not to make it personal. I first understand their point and explain my point with reasons or an example. If their approach is better, I am fine with changing my approach. The goal is to solve the problem, not to prove that I am right."

### How do you handle criticism?

"I try to separate the criticism from the emotion. I listen to what the person is saying and check whether there is something I can improve. If there is, I work on it."

### How do you handle pressure?

"I first make the problem smaller and focus on what I can control. When there is too much at once, I prioritize the important things and solve them one by one."

### How do you handle failure in a team?

"I would first focus on fixing the issue instead of finding someone to blame. After that, I would understand what caused it and what we can change so that it does not happen again."

### Tell me about a time you learned something quickly.

"When I started working with technologies like FastAPI, Redis, Celery and vector databases, I did not know all of them deeply at the beginning. I learned the basic idea first, then used each one in the project and gradually understood the details."

## Situational Questions

[Back to Table of Contents](#table-of-contents)

### What would you do if you got a task in a technology you don't know?

"I would first understand the task clearly. Then I would learn the minimum required concepts, check the existing project structure and build a small example if needed. I would ask for help when I am blocked, but I would first try to understand the problem myself."

### What if your manager gives you a deadline you think is too short?

"I would first understand the priority and expected result. If I think there is a real risk of missing the deadline, I would explain it early instead of waiting until the last moment. We can then decide what can be reduced or reprioritized."

### What if you make a mistake in production?

"I would report it quickly, help reduce the impact, understand the root cause and then work on a proper fix. I would also check whether we can add a test or some validation so the same issue does not happen again."

### What if a teammate is not contributing?

"I would first talk to them directly and understand if there is a problem. If the issue continues and affects the team, I would discuss it with the appropriate lead instead of ignoring it."

### What if your idea is rejected?

"I would ask why it was rejected and understand the reasoning. If the final decision is different from my idea, I would support the team and move forward."

### What if two tasks are both urgent?

"I would compare their impact and deadline, then clarify the priority with the person responsible if needed. I would avoid silently deciding and then missing one of them."

## Questions You Can Ask the Interviewer

[Back to Table of Contents](#table-of-contents)

At the end, ask one or two questions naturally.

### Good option 1

"Could you tell me what kind of work a fresher in this role usually gets in the first few months?"

### Good option 2

"What technologies are commonly used by the team for this role?"

### Good option 3

"What does success look like for a fresher in this role?"

### Good option 4

"How does the team usually support freshers while they are learning the project?"

Avoid ending with only:
"How much salary will I get?"

<a id="interview-traps"></a>
## Interview Traps

[Back to Table of Contents](#table-of-contents)

Avoid saying:

- "I only want Infosys because of salary."
- "TCS is not good."
- "I will definitely leave if I get a better offer."
- "I will join whatever gives me the highest package."
- "I deserve this role because I solved many questions."
- "I am a perfectionist."
- "I have no weaknesses."
- "I can work with everything."
- "I know everything about AI."
- "I don't like teamwork."
- "I just want a job."
- "I don't know" and stop there when you can explain your partial understanding.

Do not over-explain the TCS offer.

The safe structure is:

```text
Acknowledge TCS offer
        ↓
Show genuine interest in Infosys
        ↓
Explain role/learning fit
        ↓
Say you will make a thoughtful final decision
```
